# Decisions

Decisions made before this repo existed, with the reason. Read before proposing an architecture change; most of these have already been argued once. Date format: YYYY-MM-DD.

## Strategy

- **Additive streams, not phases (2026-11-03, supersedes "cash first").** The owner has one stable income stream (hourly contracting with the current agency, good rate, no sign of ending) and wants several, so that any one can shut down without consequence. Every new stream is added beside the base, never instead of it. Nothing is dropped unless something clearly better replaces it. Hourly billing stays where the hour is good; fixed-price products are added, not swapped in. Options are assumed additive unless a real resource conflict is shown; niche vs generalist, cash vs upside, cold vs referral, hourly vs fixed are run in parallel and measured separately. "Cash first" rested on a runway assumption that was wrong and is withdrawn. Full statement in PLAN.md, "The additive frame".
- **Review dates are beacons, not kill switches (2026-11-03).** Each date checks whether a stream is alive and what the reply data says. "Alive" is defined per stream by the owner before each review. Supersedes "kill dates are binding".
- **Stream 1 sells three products at once (2026-11-03):** hourly contracting (current), full-time contractor for a fixed monthly fee, fixed scope for a fixed price. The hourly rate is internal arithmetic for the fixed products and is never shown to a fixed-price buyer.
- **Three paths to a buyer, measured separately (2026-11-03):** apply to public contract postings; referral through the current agency's network, Studion alumni and past clients; direct outreach to a named engineering lead. The first contract came by referral. Reply rate per path decides the mix; no path is assumed to be the mechanism.
- **Two streams, not two niches.** Stream 1 is generalist contracting (commerce storefront lead) and needs no moat, only options. Stream 2 is agent enablement and needs a moat. They share buyers (agencies) and tooling (this repo), nothing else a prospect can see.
- **Stream 2 is agent enablement, not Salesforce Loyalty or e-invoicing.** Loyalty: a handful of job posts, learnable by a generalist in weeks. E-invoicing: real mandates, but the gap is only on custom B2B stacks after Shopify/Salesforce apps cover the easy cases, and no proof asset exists. Agent enablement is the one area where a scarce asset is already held (hardened production setup: sandboxing, `op run` secrets, cross-tool AGENTS.md, controlled sub-phases).
- **Customs-broker outbound engine dropped as a business.** No domain knowledge, no client after 6+ weeks, commodity offer. Its outreach domains and mailboxes are reused here.
- **US first (2026-11-02, reasons recorded 2026-11-03).** Better rates; much less bureaucracy for a one-person B2B company; more open to a flat monthly fee. UK probably similar, unverified. The US scores top in both rubrics (Stream 1 geography 10, Stream 2 geography 15). Supersedes "US 0 (timezone)"; the timezone objection was withdrawn. Caveats: US and UK agencies slow new contractor signings from late November to mid-January, so a quiet December is not a signal; expect C2C or vendor paperwork on the US side. US agencies are seeded from the commercetools and Salesforce Commerce directories and Clutch. Changed before an eval set exists, so rule 5 (rubric edits pass evals) could not be applied; the first eval run on 2026-11-12 labels against the new weights.
- **DACH is a weak fit (2026-11-02).** Freelance and B2B contracting in Germany, Austria and Switzerland carries heavy bureaucracy around self-employment, so DACH stays at the bottom of the Stream 1 geography band and is not a widening target. DACH agencies already seeded from partner lists stay for Stream 2, where the engagement is a fixed-price service, not a contractor placement.

## Pipeline (2026-11-02)

- **Pipeline serves every stream (2026-11-03).** Its job is to keep each stream supplied with options, not to prove one thesis. Build rows that widen options (ATS coverage, seeds, the digest) are justified on their own; rows that serve a single stream's thesis (fingerprinting, tender awards, FINA) come later or not at all. SPEC-v2.md carries the reordered build list; SPEC.md is kept as history.
- **Two routes out of the pipeline (2026-11-03).** A contract- or freelance-flagged engineering posting goes to an "apply" list: apply through the posting, no outreach, no contact discovery. Everything else goes to "outreach", which needs a named person. The engagement field in the extract schema is the switch; until extraction exists, a title keyword filter does it.
- **Fingerprinting deferred (2026-11-03).** A static fingerprint is a capability, not a signal. `platform_added` (migration in progress) points at the agency doing the build, not the retailer. Only `platform_changed` (go-live, agency rolling off) is a retailer lead, and it fires late. Kept as a later row for fixed-scope pitches, with that caveat.
- **Work arrangements the owner will take (2026-11-04).** In the owner's words: "fully remote anywhere in EU, UK, US; hybrid only if for a couple of days in office and if the salary is so big that I can afford to fly every month; never on-site except in Split, Croatia." The filter turns this into a per-posting location rule: remote in EU/UK/US is eligible; hybrid in EU/UK/US is kept and tagged `hybrid` so the owner judges the pay himself; on-site is dropped unless the location is Split; anything outside EU/UK/US is dropped. Switzerland and Norway count as in (owner confirmed 2026-11-04). An outreach signal survives only if at least one of its kept postings is eligible, so a company hiring only in India or the Philippines is dropped with reason `location`. Until 2026-11-04 the filter had no location rule at all; it was in v1 stage 3 ("countries outside scope") and was left out of the row 1 brief. A company with both offshore and onshore postings is a normal lead, not a drop: the offshore postings say how it staffs delivery, not whether it buys senior remote capacity.
- **Apply wins when a company has both (2026-11-03).** One signal per company per run; if any kept posting is contract-flagged the signal's list is `apply` and its permanent postings ride along tagged `[perm]`. A company you are applying to does not also get a cold pitch (rule 9). Routing is keyword-only (`core/filter.ts`): evergreen titles and non-engineering titles are dropped first, then contract words in the title or contract phrases in the body decide. Phrase-level on the body because "vast contract", "employment contract" and "contract testing" appear in permanent roles.
- **Contact discovery is a scoped cost, not a free step (2026-11-03).** Manual lookup of one named lead per company is allowed. Cost per 40 contacts is untimed; time the first 10 and record it here. Automated collection with the owner's own LinkedIn account is not. If automation is wanted, use a paid enrichment API keyed by company domain.
- **Automate sensing and drafting, never sending.** Final send stays human for at least v1 and v2. Reputation on the sending domain is not an expendable asset. Automated send is v3 at the earliest, after 100+ reviewed drafts.
- **Sources: ATS feeds first; fingerprinting deferred (see above). Not job boards or GitHub.** ATS per-company JSON feeds are structured, fresh, legal and under-polled by individuals. GitHub org signals were rejected: most relevant repos are private. Job boards and LinkedIn are rejected: ToS risk on the account being sold from, and recruiters have already rewritten the posting.
- **One signal per company per run.** Three postings at one company produce one signal and one draft that mentions all three. Prevents double-contacting; halves review volume.
- **Telegram digest with buttons, not a PR.** A PR was the first design. Rejected because with manual sending, merge triggers nothing, so the PR was a recording device with ceremony. Statuses in `signals` plus optional `sent_body` capture the same data. A PR becomes right only when merge means send (v3). Telegram wins on the nudge: you won't miss a 07:00 message.
- **Buttons do everything up to the send button.** Compose (`mailto:` prefilled), Rewrite, Snooze, Skip with reason. Skip reasons feed rubric tuning.
- **Local web overview with the same buttons (2026-11-04).** Added beside the Telegram digest, not instead of it. The owner had no Telegram bot yet and wanted to see and act on signals; a page served on localhost from the SQLite (`npm run ui`) shows the pending digest on top and every past signal by run date below, with Applied / Skip (with reason) / Snooze 7d / Compose. Both surfaces call the same status functions in `bot/server.ts`, so a press in either is the record. Compose is a real `mailto:` link in the browser, which Telegram cannot do. Amends "a web UI is not in v1": the nudge still comes from Telegram; the page is the desk view.
- **Secrets from a plain env file, not 1Password (2026-11-04).** The owner does not use 1Password; `op run` came from the customs-broker setup. The nightly script sources `~/.config/pipeline/env` (outside the repo, mode 600) when present, and a deployment host sets the variables on the service. The rule that stays is the one behind it: no token in any file inside this repo.
- **Instantly removed from v1 (2026-11-02):** carried over from the customs-broker setup, never decided; volume doesn't justify it. Compose via mailto only.
- **Evals before trust.** 50 hand-labeled postings per phase. Rubric edits must pass evals before commit. Skipping this is how LLM scorers rot silently.
- **The model never touches the network.** Fetching is deterministic code in stages 1–2. Headless Claude Code runs with `--allowedTools "Read,Write,Bash(sqlite3:*)"` and only reads SQLite and writes markdown.
- **SQLite committed to the repo.** Small, and history is useful. Revisit if it passes ~50 MB.
- **TypeScript on Node.** Fastest to maintain for this owner. Playwright only in `fingerprint/`, only where plain HTTP fails.
- **Builder's trap rule.** After 2026-11-16, every feature needs a number from the review to justify it. Sent messages are the only metric. If the pipeline consumes more than the protected day (1/week), it's a hobby.

## Open, deliberately

- Whether agencies that post perm roles will take a fixed-month contractor instead. Unknown until the first 20 direct messages have replies.
- The ratio of public contract postings to referral hires in the target agencies. The pipeline measures the first; the warm list measures the second.

- Whether Stream 2's agent setup is deeper than what competitors already sell (one Contra listing at $8,000/2 weeks exists). The published playbook tests this.
- Whether the Croatian market pays for Stream 2 at all. 5 of the first 15 Stream 2 messages go there.
- Exact ATS endpoint patterns and SCAYLE fingerprint hosts. Written from memory in SPEC.md; verify against live sites before building on them.
